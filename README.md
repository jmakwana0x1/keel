# keel

A Uniswap v4 hook that makes depth a contract. Depth is measured by tick-walk replay, sold to a curator, paid per block it holds, and slashed when it does not.

Status: **Phase 1 (the meter) closed at Gate 1. Phase 2 (mandate, prove, bond) in progress: core built, invariants, Halmos and testnet deploy pending.** Design decisions: [`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md). Open measurement questions: [`docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`](docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md). Phase 0 economics: [`data/phase0_results.md`](data/phase0_results.md).

Every figure below traces to a committed file in `data/`. Figures marked *machine-checked* are regenerated by a test and diffed in CI; *measured* figures come from a real chain or a fork.

## The meter (Phase 1)

`DepthLib.walk` replays the swap that moves the price to a target, one `SwapMath.computeSwapStep` per step, and reports the pre-fee input.

### Step cap and gas

`MAX_STEPS = 128`. Across the 20,700 real walks out to ±10%, the p99 was 96 steps and the max was 97. Hydra's 64 would leave 8.6% of them inconclusive (D1.1). The meter runs in `prove()`, never on the swap path.

The marginal cost is about **9,100–9,200 gas per crossing** (9,106 and 9,180) (`data/phase1_gas.json`, machine-checked; coldness guarded by `test/artifacts/GasColdness.t.sol`). ### What the verification found

A rounding bug planted inside v4-core's `SqrtPriceMath` was not caught by the meter-vs-`PoolManager` comparison on the side that compares values, because the pool and the meter share the bug. That test did fail, but only through an underflow revert on the deposit side, so the mutation would have been scored as caught for the wrong reason. Only the spec-derived Python reference caught it on the value side. That is why the second reference is a CI gate (D1.8). Full transcript: [`data/verification.md`](data/verification.md).

## The mandate (Phase 2, in progress)

`KeelHook` posts a mandate with escrow held as ERC-6909 claims, mints the curator's bands and a mint-only bond, and decides each `prove()` per D2.1. A side is compliant on the curator's closed-form contribution alone. Otherwise a pool walk decides, and a capped or proven walk is never a breach. A breach finalises only on a second breaching proof at least N blocks after the first. Any credited compliant proof inside that window closes it.

### `prove()` gas

| Path | Gas (storage priced **cold**) |
|---|---:|
| Compliant, 4 bands, first proof (zero-to-nonzero payment writes) | 133,645 |
| Same, with a ladder of initialised ticks inside the target range | 133,645 |
| Compliant, steady state (next block) | 99,445 |
| Breach, opens the window (two walks across ladder rungs) | 164,623 |
| Breach, finalises: slash, bounty, donate | 262,691 |

From `data/phase2_gas.json`, machine-checked. The artifact asserts one claim: **the compliant path runs no walk, so its gas does not depend on the book**. That assertion was observed failing (`194103 != 170388`) when a walk was planted on the compliant path. Its first version compared two books that both had initialised ticks only where the walk could not see them, and it passed with the planted walk too. It was rebuilt so the comparison is not vacuous.

The compliant path is **not** always the cheapest. A curator's first compliant proof costs more than a breach proof whose walks are short (133,475 against 121,680 at D2.8, in an earlier book with the price past the ladder), because it makes first-time zero-to-nonzero writes to the payment slots. That was the original assertion, and it was false.

### No book can put an accepted mandate beyond proof (D2.10)

**For an accepted mandate, no book exists that one segmented proof cannot cover.** That is structural. A walk to `x` stops only at multiples of the tick spacing or at the target, so its step count is bounded by `R/spacing + 3`, whatever the book holds. `post()` refuses any range above six segments of `MAX_STEPS`. Dust cannot raise the bound, so fragmentation cannot make an accepted range unprovable. The scope is exact: *accepted* mandates, *coverage*, *by fragmentation*. It does not mean a mandate is always worth slashing; the economic residual is in Limitations. It also rests on one stated assumption: the worst coverable proof is 86% of the 2^24 transaction cap, and ~89% `[modelled]` at the theoretical worst, a ~13% margin on per-crossing gas.

#### How

Dust on every tick inside the mandated range caps a single walk, and a capped walk proves nothing (rule 1). Left alone, that would keep a breach from ever finalising. So `prove(id, segments)` continues a capped walk for up to 6 segments in one call. Each segment resumes from the exact state where the last one stopped. Stitched walks equal the `PoolManager`'s own charge at **zero tolerance** across 200 fuzzed dust-ladder books (`MeterVsPoolTest.testFuzz_stitchedMatchesPool`). A proof that leaves any part of the range unwalked stays INCONCLUSIVE.

`post()` also refuses any mandate whose range could hold more steps than six segments can walk. That bound depends only on `x` and the tick spacing, never on the book, so no book can push an accepted mandate past one proof. The worst accepted case (spacing 1, ±7.35%, every tick initialised, both sides short) costs **14.41M** gas to open a window and **14.44M** to finalise, under the 16.78M EIP-7825 transaction cap (`data/phase2_segments.json`, machine-checked).

### What the Phase 2 verification found

The negative liquidity site's gate (`actor == curator && ACTIVE`, inside `_burnMandate`) could be deleted, and all 40 suite tests still passed. Both of today's callers check the same thing at their entry points, so the gate is redundant through the public API. It exists for an entry point nobody has written yet. `test/unit/NegativeSite.t.sol` now calls the site directly, and a deleted gate fails two tests by name (`ManagerLocked() != NotCurator()`). A control test, in which the curator of an ACTIVE mandate passes the gate, keeps passing. The symbolic version is `check_mandateDeltaRequiresActiveCurator` (Halmos, pending). Full transcripts: [`data/verification.md`](data/verification.md).

## Limitations

Stated before anyone has to find them.

- **No measured price for long-tail mandates** (D1.5). Phase 0 could not size a valid simulated band on long-tail books (D0.15). The hook accepts long-tail pools. The reserve there will start from a stated prior, marked as a prior, and be replaced by the pool's own realised cost as mandates complete (D1.6, not yet built).
- **The skim's elasticity is associational, not causal** (D0.5, D0.10). The skim is restored at the configuration that clears break-even under a 75% discount of ε (s = 5%, φ = 10%, D1.4). It is not yet built.
- **Liveness is assumed** (D1.3). A curator who is compliant but never proves inside a staleness window can be slashed for a stretch that was in fact compliant. Proving is cheap on the compliant path, and a window gives N blocks of notice, but nobody proves for you.
- **Pay scales with proving cadence** (D2.2). One compliant proof pays one block, so a curator who wants every block paid proves every block. That suits a cheap-block chain and not mainnet.
- **A slash freezes the curator's principal** (D2.5). The slash itself takes accrued value only. But negative liquidity deltas are allowed only while a mandate is ACTIVE, so a slashed curator's bands stay in the pool permanently. That, not the accrued retainer, is the real penalty.
- **A fragmented range can still be uneconomic to prove** (D2.10). The bounty is 20% of accrued. A worst-case fragmented breach costs a watcher up to ~28.9M gas across the opening and finalising proofs. So a third-party watcher profits only when `accrued > 1.44 × 10⁸ · gasPrice`. That is about 0.00014 ETH at 0.001 gwei and 1.44 ETH at 10 gwei `[modelled]` (gas prices are assumptions; the gas is measured). Below that, the attack no longer works technically but still works economically. When `accrued` is zero, nobody has a bounty on any book. The issuer, refunded at a slash, is then the watcher of last resort.
- **Fine-spacing pools take narrower mandates** (D2.10). At tick spacing 1, `x` is capped at 7.35% so that every accepted range stays provable in one transaction.
- **Phase 2 scope cuts** (D2.6): one live mandate per pool, ERC-20 pools only, a fixed retainer rate, and no skim yet.
